Engineering research · Unpublished manuscript draft

Fast to start.
Reliable to stop?

An offline replay benchmark of filtering commands decoded from muscle signals.

Try the replay lab ↗Read the draftCode on GitHub ↗

The question

A filter can suppress a short unwanted command. But waiting for more evidence can also delay a correct movement—or keep a command active after it should stop.

I tested whether selecting activation and release persistence separately helped under explicit requirements for onset, release, and active coverage.

What was built

Causal trailing-window features, personalized decoders, seven controller families, a frozen DB5 protocol, 23 sensitivity cases, and a native-rate DB2 replication.

What happened

The original hypothesis was unsupported.

The tested controller grids did not meet the combined development requirements. The primary asymmetric fallback increased command exposure during annotated rest.

This is a documented negative result. The contribution is the benchmark and its evaluation of the tradeoffs.
