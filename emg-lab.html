How this demo is scored

Each decision is held for 25 ms. The movement annotation is grip from 1,000 to 3,000 ms, with rest before and after. Onset and release require 100 ms of stable output: five decision endpoints. Confirmation is recorded at the fifth endpoint, never backdated to the start.

Onset is confirmed correct grip within 250 ms of the annotation boundary. Release is confirmed rest within 150 ms of the offset. Coverage, correct output, and wrong active output are measured from 1,500 to 3,000 ms. Coverage counts any nonzero command, including wrong ones. Unwanted activation is nonzero-command time divided by all annotated rest time.

This toy stream has one event and three labels. It has no EMG feature window, decoder uncertainty, or participant sampling. The hands display the current command immediately without adding simulated motor delay; they do not model force, biomechanics, or hardware response. Single-event timing checks are not the study’s participant-level feasibility test. The browser controllers and stable-confirmation routine are checked against the benchmark’s Python implementation.

No feasible improvement was established.

All development controller families were infeasible under the joint requirements. All 14 evaluated DB5 methods had 0/8 joint successes; all 19 DB2 methods had 0/32. These contrasts and bootstrap intervals are descriptive after failed development feasibility.

Offline intact-limb replay is not a clinical or closed-loop prosthetic trial.
